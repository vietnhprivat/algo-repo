\begin{abstract}
Day-ahead electricity price forecasting (EPF) research has produced accurate
benchmark models but evaluates them offline, stopping short of the concerns ---
scheduled retraining, monitoring, and reproducibility --- that arise when a model
is operated in production. This thesis bridges that gap by embedding the
benchmark EPF models of Lago et al.\ (the LEAR ensemble and a deep neural
network) and the Transformer of Llorente and Portela in an end-to-end MLOps
platform, and evaluating both the forecasts and the system that serves them. The
platform ingests ENTSO-E data on a schedule, stores it in ClickHouse,
recalibrates and forecasts daily for the DK1 and DE bidding zones, monitors data
and model health, and records the provenance of every run. The three models are
replicated to specification and compared on a shared recent test window with
paired Diebold--Mariano and Giacomini--White significance tests:
\textbf{[pending: one-sentence forecasting headline]}. The system is assessed
against the nine MLOps practices of Zarour et al.: eight are realised end-to-end
and one is minimally realised, supported by an ML Test Score of 2.5 (bound by
data testing), 66.7\,\% provenance-triple coverage backed by a re-run that
reproduced a logged forecast to within $7\times10^{-6}$\,EUR/MWh, and DORA
throughput of 20 deployments at a sub-minute lead time. The outcome is a
reproducible, monitored EPF platform together with a faithful,
significance-tested model comparison on current market data.
\end{abstract}
