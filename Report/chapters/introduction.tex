Electricity price forecasting (EPF) is the task of predicting day-ahead
wholesale electricity prices for a given bidding zone from a combination of
historical prices and exogenous signals such as load, renewable generation, and
calendar effects. Research on EPF has produced increasingly accurate offline
benchmarks — LEAR, deep neural networks, and hybrid ensembles — evaluated on
fixed historical holdout periods under idealised conditions.

Production EPF, however, imposes a different and harder set of requirements.
A deployed forecasting system must continuously ingest fresh market data from
external APIs, retrain or recalibrate models on a rolling basis as the price
distribution shifts, monitor prediction quality in near-real time, and align
the evaluation methodology with actual operational use — a contrast that the
EPF research literature largely sidesteps in favour of reporting accuracy on
offline benchmarks \cite{lago2021}.
